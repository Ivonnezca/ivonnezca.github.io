About

My research focuses on understanding the fundamental structure of the Universe. I want to understand the origin and evolution of the Universe and some of its biggest mysteries: what drove the accelerated expansion of the very early Universe (inflation), what drives the acceleration we see today (dark energy), and what dark matter is. My aim is to understand how these phenomena can be explained by a fundamental theory of gravity such as string theory.

I also want to find out whether, and how, such fundamental theories can be tested with the latest observations. That means cosmological data, and also gravitational waves, especially those of primordial origin. These will become accessible in the coming years with LISA (the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna) and the Einstein Telescope, and pulsar timing arrays such as NANOGrav are already probing them. To get there I use analytic and numerical techniques and, more recently, machine learning.

Research

String Phenomenology

How does the geometry of extra dimensions shape the physics of our four-dimensional world?

Moduli, supergravity and quantum gravity

Warped throats, anti-branes and non-linear supergravity, and the swampland conjectures that constrain which effective theories can come from quantum gravity.

Heterotic orbifolds and their symmetries

Coupling selection rules, discrete R-symmetries and modular symmetry, and moduli stabilisation in heterotic string models close to the Standard Model.

What if our Universe is a brane in a spacetime with more dimensions?

Brane worlds and self-tuning

Supersymmetric large extra dimensions

Warped and axisymmetric solutions of six-dimensional chiral gauged supergravity (SLED), their stability, and what they say about the cosmological constant problem.

String Cosmology

Can the Universe we observe emerge from string theory?

Inflation from strings and branes

Building inflation from D-branes, warped throats and string axions, and asking what string compactifications predict for primordial black holes and gravitational waves.

Time-dependent solutions in supergravity and string theory

S-branes and other time-dependent backgrounds as models of cosmological spacetimes: bouncing brane worlds, negative-tension branes and particle production.

Dark energy and de Sitter in string theory

Whether string theory admits de Sitter vacua or quintessence, how well controlled those constructions are, and how to confront them with data.

Cosmological Inflation

What happened in the first instant, when several fields shaped the expansion?

Multifield inflation and turning trajectories

Observational signatures

Non-Gaussianity, reheating, the inflaton field range and tensor modes, and what CMB data can and cannot tell us about the inflationary potential.

The Dark Universe

What are dark energy and dark matter, and what can the newest data tell us about them?

Dark energy meets data

Quintessence motivated by quantum gravity, bounded and phantom-crossing dark energy, and data-driven reconstructions with Bayesian and machine-learning tools.

Coupled dark sectors and dark matter

Interactions between dark energy and dark matter, multi-field dynamical systems, and how dark matter relics depend on the expansion history of the early Universe.

Opening doors to physics

Making physics open to everyone matters to me. I am proud to supervise Eleanna Kolonia, the first Bell Burnell Graduate Scholar in Swansea Physics. The scholarship, created by Dame Jocelyn Bell Burnell through the Institute of Physics, supports PhD students from groups that are under-represented in physics.

Join us

We welcome PhD students, postdocs and visitors who want to work on string cosmology, string phenomenology, dark energy or gravitational waves. When we have funded positions in the Theoretical Cosmology and Gravity group we advertise them on INSPIRE Jobs.

We are also glad to host researchers who bring their own funding, and to help strong candidates prepare fellowship applications with Swansea as host. Get in touch early, ideally a few months before the deadline.
